# Travian rewards raiders; Stellar Syndicates rewards producers

In Travian: Legends, raiding other players is the best-paying use of an attentive player's first month, and a supplement after that. A raiding unit repays its training cost in **about 12–30 hours of full-load raiding at 10–20 fields**, while a resource field needs 2.6 days at level 1 and two weeks at level 10. The best documented hauls ran at **roughly 10–16 times a young village's output**, in line with the community rule of 10–20× field income in a server's first month. The edge is front-loaded and limited by targets: it feeds on inactive accounts and on stock above the cranny, needs hourly sends and the paid Gold Club farm list, shrinks to **under 0.6× production** for a 30-village account, and guide writers stop farming players around weeks 4–5. Waiting is not neutral either, because an idle player's overflow is exactly what raiders take. Stellar Syndicates' current rules sit at the opposite pole. One Combat Freighter load is worth **3.5–27 minutes of a single home mine**. Stripping a whole home store needs a declared war (live two hours after it lands) or a reprisal right, plus a beaten defence, and returns **half an hour to two hours of the victim's income**. The loot flies home in a hull that can itself be raided, burning fuel that can cost more than an ore cargo is worth, and a home Mining Complex I repays its goods bill in about eight minutes. In SS, producing beats raiding by an order of magnitude or more, raiding other players serves denial and reprisal rather than income, and NPC pirates, not players, do the routine farming of absent players, capped at about 15–17% of one mine's output. The Travian multiples rest on pre-Legends anecdotes and an undated wiki, because no Legends raid statistics are public. Every SS figure is derived from code constants rather than measured play.

## A Travian raider repays itself in hours, a field in weeks

A Travian unit returning full earns its carry capacity times its speed, divided by twice the distance, every hour, so raid income halves each time the target distance doubles ([kirilloid distance model](http://travian.kirilloid.ru/js/distance.js)). On an x1 server the best raiders earn **about 70–90 resources an hour each at 10 fields**: the Spartan Elpida Rider 88, the Hun Marksman 79 and the Gaul Theutates Thunder 71. Cheap infantry earns 15–21 ([kirilloid unit data](http://travian.kirilloid.ru/js/units.js)). A better measure of how much income a raider buys is carry per 100 resources of training cost. The Clubswinger (24) and Thrall (21) lead, cavalry sits at 7–11 and heavy hitters at 3–5. That is why the Help Center markets Teutons as cheap, quick to train and the best carriers in the game ([Official: Teutons](https://support.travian.com/en/articles/8-the-teutons-and-their-advantages)). These unit figures come from kirilloid's community-built model. The Help Center's Vikings table matches it on every value it publishes, but no official Legends table lists carry capacity.

All four field types follow one production curve in Legends: 7 an hour at level 1, 280 at level 10 and 3,430 at level 20. Costs rise ×1.67 a level while output rises about ×1.4, so each level repays more slowly than the last ([kirilloid Legends buildings](https://github.com/kirilloid/travian/blob/master/src/model/t4/buildings.ts); [kirilloid base buildings](https://github.com/kirilloid/travian/blob/master/src/model/base/buildings.ts)). Side by side, the gap is stark:

| Asset (x1, no bonuses) | Cost (resources) | Income per hour | Payback |
|---|---|---|---|
| Clubswinger raiding at 10 fields | 250 | 21 | 12 h |
| Marksman raiding at 10 fields | 1,050 | 79 | 13 h |
| Theutates Thunder raiding at 10 fields | 1,090 | 71 | 15 h |
| Clubswinger raiding at 20 fields | 250 | 10.5 | 24 h |
| Legionnaire raiding at 10 fields | 400 | 15 | 27 h |
| Resource field, level 1 | 250 | +4 | 62 h (2.6 days) |
| Resource field, level 5 | 1,945 | +15 | 130 h (5.4 days) |
| Resource field, level 10 | 25,255 | +77 | 328 h (13.7 days) |
| Capital field, level 15 | 328,075 | +231 | 1,420 h (59 days) |

*Computed in the research notes from kirilloid's unit and building models. Raid rows assume every trip returns full, with no losses and no idle time.*

A Clubswinger farming at 10 fields repays itself **about five times faster than a level-1 field and nearly thirty times faster than a level-10 field**. A level-20 capital field needs 282 days, longer than the 250 days after which an x1 round's Natar Wonder completes ([Official: Game Versions and Speed](https://support.travian.com/en/articles/20-game-versions-and-speed)). The raid rows are ceilings, not observations: no source measures how often real trips come home full. Two features of current Legends also lift the "wait and build" side. First, the hero. Its four starting points now produce 9 of each resource per point, **144 an hour against 54 from a new village's fields**, triple an older published rate. Pre-Legends guides that dismiss passive income therefore understate it ([Official: Hero in the early game](https://support.travian.com/en/articles/141-hero-in-the-early-game); [CodeGuru forum](https://forums.codeguru.com/showthread.php?p=2076569)). Second, server speed. Faster servers multiply production by the full speed factor but troop speed only ×2 on x2–x5 and ×4 on x10 ([Official: Game Versions and Speed](https://support.travian.com/en/articles/20-game-versions-and-speed)). Measured against fields, raiding keeps its full appeal on x2, two-thirds of it on x3 and two-fifths on x5 and x10.

## Top raiders out-earn their fields 10–20× for about a month

Legends treats loot as income in its own bookkeeping. The anti-pushing limits add each player's seven-day average hourly raid income to their production when setting how much they may send to other players ([Official: Automatic resource-sharing limits](https://support.travian.com/en/articles/125-automatic-resource-sharing-limits)). A weekly Top-10 Raiders board also awards medals every Monday ([Official: How Medals work](https://support.travian.com/en/articles/168-how-medals-work)). No Legends-era values from that board are publicly archived, so the best figures are older. A Travian 4 Gaul on the uk2 server in 2013/14 reached 300 Theutates Thunders and finished week 1 as the #5 raider with **500,245 resources (about 3,000 an hour)**. In week 2 he was #3 with **805,308 (about 4,800 an hour)**. His own sizing table puts a 100-population village at about 300 an hour, so he was banking **roughly 10–16 times a young village's output** ([T4 Start Game Guide](https://lordsoftravian.forumotion.com/t5-t4-start-game-guide)). The Travian Wiki generalises that in a speed server's first month "top Teutons can earn ten to twenty times their field income". The page is undated and was read only through search excerpts ([Travian Wiki: Farm](https://travian.fandom.com/wiki/Farm)). A Legends-era r/travian post from October 2026, also seen only as an excerpt, still ranks raiding as the best income source, with a far higher ceiling than pure building ([r/travian mirror](https://r.datuan.dev/r/travian/comments/1uhrwxl/noob_advice/)).

Even at face value, the anecdote shows that the supply of targets, not carry capacity, set the ceiling. Running full, his 300 Thunders could have carried about 21,000 an hour at 10 fields or 10,800 at 20. He banked at most 4,800, a quarter to a half of capacity. That is my arithmetic: the server speed is unstated, and a speed server would lower the share further. A 2009 guide, seen only in an excerpt, likewise puts the gap between a top-10 and a top-100 raider down to how often troops are sent: 50 or more raids kept in flight for 48 hours straight ([Travian Library: Growth Guide](https://travianlibrary.wordpress.com/2009/03/13/growth-guide/)). Targets run out. Inactive accounts are the standard farm, guides keep known active players off farm lists altogether, and Travian 4 deleted abandoned starts after about a week ([T4 Start Game Guide](https://lordsoftravian.forumotion.com/t5-t4-start-game-guide)). An unoccupied oasis regenerates only **71–111 resources an hour** at x1, enough to keep about three Clubswingers busy ([Official: Oasis](https://support.travian.com/en/articles/48-oasis)).

Over a server's life the multiple then falls by more than an order of magnitude. The same wiki page puts a 30-village account above half a million resources an hour of production, with top raiders adding 300,000 or more. That is **under 0.6× production, about a third of total income**. It fits an official 2023 blog benchmark for an x2 server: a fully developed village costs about 1.35 million resources, yields 16,800 an hour and repays itself in about 80 hours ([official blog, reposted](https://unofficialtravian.com/2025/01/early-development-return-on-investment/)). Thirty such villages make about 504,000 an hour. A single one, about 8,400 an hour at x1 if output scales with speed, already out-produces the week-2 #3 raider. The anecdote's author stops farming players around week 5 and puts the last weeks' loot into fields. A Travian 4 Teuton guide sends most early loot into the second village ([T4 Teuton Full Guide](http://travian4guides.blogspot.com/p/t4-teuton-full-guide.html)). Raiding's lasting payoff is compounding: loot taken in weeks 1–4 becomes fields and villages that keep producing after the farms run dry.

The designers spell out the same trade-off. The 2023 blog says an army must earn enough to cover its own training and upkeep. It suggests farming accounts reinvest farm income roughly 60% into troops and 40% into the economy, and tells players who don't farm to wait for two fully developed villages before training troops. Legends also gave armies a target that doesn't feed on other players. Since around 2021–23, every oasis animal killed pays the hero a bounty, from 40 of each resource for a rat to 200 for an elephant, even if every attacker dies ([Official: Early Game Oasis Farming](https://support.travian.com/en/articles/190-early-game-oasis-farming)). The Help Center ranks this among the most impactful changes in Legends. It advises sending troops only when their losses cost at most half of what the hero gains ([Official: Oasis Farming](https://support.travian.com/en/articles/185-oasis-farming-after-beginner-protection)).

## Raiding costs attention and gold; waiting costs your overflow

Raids never wipe out the attacker, but they always cost something. In the community combat model, a raider loses 1/(1+x) of its force, where x is its offence over the target's defence, raised to the power 1.5. That comes to **26% at a 2:1 strength edge, 8% at 5:1 and 3% at 10:1** ([kirilloid combat model](https://github.com/kirilloid/travian/blob/master/src/model/base/combat/combat.ts)). Villages with no troops at home still fight back, because walls and a Residence or Palace add passive defence: 800 points for a level-20 Palace ([Official: Battle Mechanics](https://support.travian.com/en/articles/63-battle-mechanics)). Ten unupgraded Clubswingers raiding a village with no troops lose about 18% to a level-10 Roman wall and about 74% to a level-20 Palace. Two hundred lose about 3% against the same Palace, which is why raiders send far more troops than the loot needs. Losses cut income twice, since dead units carry nothing home. Gaul Trappers hold up to 400 attackers each at level 20, and no raid can free them ([Official: Trapper](https://support.travian.com/en/articles/41-trapper)). Every unit eats crop by the hour: 500 infantry at one crop each consume about 30% of the crop an all-level-10 village grows.

Raiding at scale also costs gold and attention. The Farm List comes only with the Gold Club, a one-time 200 Gold for the round ([Official: Gold Club](https://support.travian.com/en/articles/128-gold-club-overview); [Official: Farm Lists](https://support.travian.com/en/articles/69-farm-lists)), and sending stays manual. The anecdote's author spent about three hours building his lists and sent raids every hour, and a Teuton guide recommends two sitters (players trusted to log in for you) to keep raids running overnight. Returns therefore concentrate among players who are online a lot and often pay.

The other side is that waiting is not safe. Production above storage capacity is lost while the fields keep running. Everything above the cranny (a building that hides resources from raiders) can be looted, including the warehouse, the granary and open market offers ([Official: Resources Explained](https://support.travian.com/en/articles/214-guide-resources-explained)). A level-10 cranny hides 2,000 of each resource (3,000 for Gauls), and a player may build more than one. A Teuton hero riding with the raid cuts its protection by a fifth ([Official: Cranny](https://support.travian.com/en/articles/35-hiding-resources-and-cranny)). The 8,000 resources a level-10 cranny shelters cover about a day of output for a young village making 300 an hour, but under two hours for an all-level-10 village. The cranny therefore shields newcomers who spend what they make, and matters less as output grows. Beginner's protection runs 5 days plus an optional 3 at x1 and cannot be ended early ([Official: Beginner's Protection](https://support.travian.com/en/articles/12-beginner-s-protection)). After it ends, the morale bonus that once shielded smaller players is gone (removed in 2022) and no other small-player rule replaces it. What remains is the cranny, walls, spending, vacation days and alliances. The players who lose most are the ones who log in least, the post-protection stretch that commentators call Early Game Hell ([TV Tropes: Travian](https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/VideoGame/Travian)).

Legends has left raiding's ceiling intact. Its documented changes made raiding easier and better tracked: a farm-list overhaul that shows income figures and adds a "Start all" button, a wave builder at 50 Gold per village, and a weekly ranking split into PvE and PvP. Meanwhile, the anti-pushing rules restrict raids between linked accounts ([Unofficial Travian: 6 years of changes](https://unofficialtravian.com/2025/01/travian-legends-6-years-of-changes-at-a-glance/)). The research found no Legends cap on loot taken from other players. The sister game Travian Kingdoms added one on selected special worlds: a daily limit that starts at **30,000 in week 1 at 3× speed and climbs to 450,000 from week 7**, with any excess left with the defender, to balance raiding ([Kingdoms: Loot limit](https://support.kingdoms.com/en/articles/187-loot-limit)). It is the publisher's clearest signal that uncapped raiding early in a server needs a brake.

## A Syndicates raid takes minutes of the victim's output

Stellar Syndicates runs in real time, one sim second per wall-clock second ([game_loop.rs:42](../crates/server/src/game_loop.rs#L42)). It lets players take each other's goods by three built routes, each narrower than a Travian raid.

**Convoy raid.** An Intercept order on an unescorted freighter needs no war. The raid ends after 0.35 of the 45-second battle length, about 16 seconds, and seizes the manifest only if every freighter hull dies. The loot fills the raider's own freighter holds and the rest sinks with the wreck. A Corvette escort or a covering defence platform turns it into an ordinary battle instead ([combat.rs:72-77](../crates/sim/src/combat.rs#L72); [world.rs:5103-5140](../crates/sim/src/world.rs#L5103); [world.rs:13873-13895](../crates/sim/src/world.rs#L13873)). It costs no Authority standing, but it gives the victim a 12-hour right of reprisal ([diplomacy.rs:23-26](../crates/sim/src/diplomacy.rs#L23)).

**Blockade plunder.** This needs a declared war, live two hours after the declaration reaches its target, or a live reprisal right ([diplomacy.rs:14-17](../crates/sim/src/diplomacy.rs#L14); [world.rs:6687-6706](../crates/sim/src/world.rs#L6687)). Nothing is taken while a station hardpoint, a covering warship or a fed garrison still contests the system. Once the blockade holds, the besieger strips **1.5 units a second, most valuable good first, never below 40 of any good**. The goods go into freighter holds only; warships carry none ([docs/blockades.md:25-55](../docs/blockades.md#L25); [world.rs:765-783](../crates/sim/src/world.rs#L765)).

**Siege capture.** This takes colonies, never homes, after 6 h × (1 + 0.3 × garrison), and hands over the whole stockpile and the structures at half their tier ([world.rs:14814-14892](../crates/sim/src/world.rs#L14814)).

A code comment states the intent. A raid order against stockpiles would be "strictly better than chasing convoys", so convoys stay the fast target and stockpiles the committed one ([world.rs:751-764](../crates/sim/src/world.rs#L751)).

The dedicated raiding hull is the Combat Freighter, built Oct 27 and deployed to production Oct 28. It has an **80-unit hold that takes only loot** (120 with Prize Holds research), speed 70, no guns and no transponder, and it costs about 1,970 Cr of goods and 20 seconds at Shipyard II ([build.rs:795-807](../crates/sim/src/build.rs#L795); [docs/blockades.md:113-138](../docs/blockades.md#L113); [combat-freighter memory note](/Users/peter/.claude/projects/-Users-peter-projects-stellar-syndicates/memory/combat-freighter.md)). A home Mining Complex I makes 23 Ferrite Ore a minute, about 11,000 Cr an hour at the Hub's base price of 8 ([docs/founding-charter.md:204-205](../docs/founding-charter.md#L204); [market.rs:141-201](../crates/sim/src/market.rs#L141)). One full load is therefore worth **640 Cr of ore to 4,960 Cr of Machinery: 3.5 to 27 minutes of one home mine**. Against the 24–38k Cr/h a bot test run measured for home planet mining on Oct 10, it is 1–12 minutes. That figure predates the fuel overhaul, the bare start and the workforce cap ([docs/asteroid-belt-mining-plan.md:272-294](../docs/asteroid-belt-mining-plan.md#L272)). Stripping a whole home store doesn't change the order of magnitude. A home at the end of the founding charter holds only 1,300–2,000 units under one shared cap, and production stops when the cap is reached ([build.rs:1685-1707](../crates/sim/src/build.rs#L1685)). A full strip takes about 20 minutes at 1.5 units a second and yields roughly 16,000–50,000 Cr, which is **half an hour to two hours of the victim's income**, and only after a war declaration and a beaten defence.

The trip carries costs Travian never charges. Fuel burns in proportion to mass times distance, and each unit of cargo adds 28 mass ([fuel.rs:43-46](../crates/sim/src/fuel.rs#L43); [ship.rs:154-157](../crates/sim/src/ship.rs#L154)). An Interceptor and a Combat Freighter flying between neighbouring homes 60,000 su apart burn about 80 Deuterium (about 1,100 Cr) on full warp, or 20 Uranium (about 110 Cr) on slow warp ([galaxy.rs:1491-1495](../crates/sim/src/galaxy.rs#L1491)). An ore load loses money at full warp. The loaded freighter can itself be raided on the way home. A convoy raid also destroys more than it moves. A Small Freighter carrying 150 Alloys yields 80 Alloys (2,080 Cr) to one Combat Freighter, while its owner loses about 6,500 Cr of cargo and hull. The Boarding Parties research (Corsair V) narrows that gap: the stripped freighters join the raider as a prize fleet at a quarter hull ([world.rs:5710-5755](../crates/sim/src/world.rs#L5710)). Production capital, by contrast, costs almost nothing in goods:

| Asset (base prices, before freight) | Goods cost | Return | Payback |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mining Complex I at home | about 1,394 Cr (12 Machinery, 25 Alloys) | about 11,000 Cr/h of ore | about 8 minutes |
| Interceptor + Combat Freighter, Machinery loads | about 3,950 Cr | about 3,860–4,850 Cr net per load | 1 load |
| Same, Ferrite loads, slow warp | about 3,950 Cr | about 530 Cr net per load | 7–8 loads |
| Same, Ferrite loads, full warp | about 3,950 Cr | about −460 Cr per load | never |

*My arithmetic from the recipes ([build.rs:931-934](../crates/sim/src/build.rs#L931)), base prices and fuel constants. Real sales land below base price, and every raid load also needs a war or reprisal right and a beaten defence.*

What actually limits SS production is workforce, not goods: the charter's 6,000 settlers staff exactly six posts ([docs/founding-charter.md:38-45](../docs/founding-charter.md#L38)). Absent players are farmed by NPC pirates under tight caps. A pirate home raid takes at most 60 units and a tenth of each good, and never takes a good below 40. It waits 30 minutes after a corporation joins, warns before setting out, leaves 15 minutes between raids on the same victim, and steals nothing while a defence platform tier or one of the owner's combat ships is in range to cover the home ([pirate.rs:101-112](../crates/sim/src/pirate.rs#L101); [pirate_home_raids.rs:9-58](../crates/sim/src/world/pirate_home_raids.rs#L9)). On the dev server, a corporation whose owner was away and which had no defences was raided about every 17 minutes, 427 times in total ([blockades-running memory note](/Users/peter/.claude/projects/-Users-peter-projects-stellar-syndicates/memory/blockades-running.md)). That is a drain of **210–240 units an hour, 15–17% of one Mining Complex I**. Beating pirates is SS's PvE income. It pays Authority bounties of 600–1,100 Cr per hideout and 3,000–8,000 Cr for depots and strongholds, counter-raid bounties of 400 Cr per defence tier, the charter's 4,500 Cr privateer bounty and the vendetta's 6,000 Cr ([world.rs:20650-20664](../crates/sim/src/world.rs#L20650); [counter_raids.rs:10-75](../crates/sim/src/world/counter_raids.rs#L10); [founding.rs:96-100](../crates/sim/src/founding.rs#L96)).

## Four rule choices invert Travian's raid economy

| Rule | Travian: Legends (x1) | Stellar Syndicates (Oct 28 2026) |
|---|---|---|
| New-player shield | 5 days, +3 optional; cannot be ended early | 24 h minimum, 48 h ceiling; can be dropped from 30 min |
| Before taking a stockpile | any unprotected player, no declaration | a war live 2 h after it lands, or a 12 h reprisal right |
| Raidable home store | everything above the cranny; storage 11,800 (L10) to 80,000 (L20) per resource | 1,300–2,000 units in all at a charter-end home |
| Protected minimum | 2,000 per resource per level-10 cranny (Gauls 3,000); more crannies allowed | 40 units per good; the Hub warehouse can never be raided |
| Loot per raider | 60 per Clubswinger, 75 per Theutates Thunder | 80 per Combat Freighter (120 with Prize Holds); warships none |
| Cost per trip | none beyond hourly crop upkeep | fuel ∝ mass × distance; about 1,100 Cr at full warp between neighbours |
| Loot on the way home | resources in transit cannot be stolen | rides in a freighter that can be raided |
| Away protection | vacation, 15 days a round | Leave, 2–15 days, 7-day cooldown; also stops pirate home raids |
| Automation | Farm List in the Gold Club (200 Gold) | none; a Raid order is planned |
| Loot cap | none in Legends | store size, 40-unit floor, war gate |
| Who farms absent players | other players | NPC pirates, at most 60 units a raid |
| PvE alternative | oasis animal bounty, 40–200 per resource per animal | pirate bounties 600–8,000 Cr, counter-raids, salvage |

*Sources as cited in the sections above, plus [Official: Interacting with Other Players](https://support.travian.com/en/articles/11-interacting-with-other-players), [founding.rs:13-22](../crates/sim/src/founding.rs#L13) and [leave.rs:16-20](../crates/sim/src/leave.rs#L16).*

**Wealth sits in motion, not in storage.** A Travian village stores hours to days of output. A level-10 warehouse holds about 10.5 hours of an all-level-10 village's wood, a level-20 one about three days, and the raider skims what builds up. An SS home holds about 1.5 hours of one mine's output and production stops at the cap, so value has to move. It goes either to the Hub, whose warehouse can never be blockaded and sits inside a 900 su zone where no fight can start ([tca.rs:70](../crates/sim/src/tca.rs#L70); [docs/engagement-rules.md:29-31](../docs/engagement-rules.md#L29)), or out through convoys. SS has moved the raid target from the stockpile to the supply line. As a side effect, the Hub warehouse works as an unlimited cranny.

**Theft needs notice and invites reprisal.** In Travian anyone can raid any unprotected player without declaring anything, and the only penalty is retaliation. In SS no blockade can surprise a defender, because the war goes live two hours after it lands. A convoy raid on a corporation you are not at war with is legal at any time, but it gives the victim twelve hours in which it can blockade the raider in turn. SS's founder protection is shorter than Travian's (24–48 hours against 5–8 days at x1) and can be dropped early. Even so, the war requirement protects players more than any Travian rule does.

**Trips cost fuel, and loot can be taken back.** Travian troops eat crop whether or not they move, and nothing in transit can be stolen ([Official: Resources Explained](https://support.travian.com/en/articles/214-guide-resources-explained)). SS charges fuel for every trip and turns every loaded raider into a target, which makes cheap goods not worth stealing at full warp.

**SS limits how often you can raid, not what each raid pays.** Per load, SS raid hulls repay about as fast as Travian's units: one to eight loads, against about four for a Clubswinger (250 cost, 60 carry) and fifteen for a Theutates Thunder (1,090 cost, 75 carry). The gap is frequency. A Travian top-10 raider is defined by send volume, with dozens of farms hit every hour from one list. An SS stockpile raid needs a war or reprisal right, a beaten defence and a victim whose store holds about 1.5 hours of output. SS's pace widens the gap further. Tier I–II structures build in seconds and a Mining Complex repays in minutes, while stockpile theft waits on a two-hour war notice. Travian has the same effect on its fast servers, where production speeds up more than troop travel, but SS pushes it much further.

The one SS route that resembles Travian farming is a held blockade on a system that keeps producing. Outbound shipping waits at the origin, and nothing in the documented blockade rules stops the victim's mines. In principle, then, a besieger with enough hold room collects the system's ongoing output as well as its store, at a strip rate about four times a home mine's. That is occupation, not a raid: warships must stay on station for as long as it runs.

The planned changes map onto Travian's levers one for one, and none is built. A Raid order (blockade rules, load until full or contested, return automatically) would be SS's farm list. Sealed stores, meaning Warehouses that raise the protected floor, would be its cranny. Neither has numbers yet ([blockades-running memory note](/Users/peter/.claude/projects/-Users-peter-projects-stellar-syndicates/memory/blockades-running.md)). The R11 plan ramps pirate raids by season week, from 10% of each good's stock to 27.5% in week 8, with a largest raid of 390 units ([docs/r11-pirate-pressure-plan.md](../docs/r11-pirate-pressure-plan.md#L1)). That is the same shape as Kingdoms' loot limit, which also rises with the world's age.

## Conclusion

Travian's raid premium comes from its conditions, not from raiding itself: deep unspent stock from inactive players and overflow, free trips with loot that can't be taken back, and unlimited, unannounced repeat raids through farm lists. As those conditions wear away (crannies, deleted accounts, developed villages, faster servers), the premium falls from 10–20× toward 0.6×. Stellar Syndicates removed all three by rule, so its raiders start where Travian's end up, at or below the late-game supplement. Under the current rules a rational SS player farms pirates rather than players. Raids on other players serve denial and reprisal, such as sinking a rival's freight or squeezing a colony. Travian-scale theft is reachable only by occupying a producing system.

That reframes the open decisions. SS raid hulls already repay in one to eight loads, so a Raid order's economics turn on how often it can run, not on how much each load pays. If it lets a player repeat stockpile raids automatically within a war, it becomes the farm-list lever that made Travian raiding dominant, and it decides whether a player-raider playstyle exists at all. Sealed stores only tighten a system that is already tight. Travian's own history, from Early Game Hell complaints to a Kingdoms loot limit that starts low and rises with server age, argues for raiding pressure that grows with world age and stays below the victim's production. That is the shape R11 already proposes for pirates, and the yardstick to size any Raid order against.
